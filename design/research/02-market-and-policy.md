# Throw / collect / idle-income genre: market and policy findings (snapshot 2026-11-14)

## Verdict in one line
The throw-to-collect idle loop is live and huge (Throw Food For Animals is roughly 200M+ visits and 300K+ CCU two months after release), but the anime-character angle is only lightly tested (the closest anime idle titles are small or dead), and the IP risk for recognisable anime characters is real: Roblox removes IP-infringing content on DMCA notice and does not reopen it without the rights holder's consent. An original-character anime look with a licensed-or-original roster is the defensible version.

### B. Monetisation
5. Gamepass and product prices with Robux, and which sell best: UNKNOWN. I did not find a developer talk or analytics write-up with sales data by product type in the opened pages.
6. Policy constraints (verified on official docs):
   - Paid random items: all possible outcomes and their actual numerical odds must be shown before purchase, summing to 100%. This also covers indirect purchases (keys, spin tickets). A bare (i) icon is not enough. Luck boosts that change odds must explain their effect before purchase and update live. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/monetization/paid-random-items
   - ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted (from PolicyService:GetPolicyInfoForPlayerAsync): when true, the user cannot use paid random generators with Robux or Robux-bought currency. Compliance options are: free earnable route, fixed disclosed order, sell the outcome directly at expected value, remove the item, block with an error, or move the user out of the area. The same API returns IsPaidItemTradingAllowed. Source: same URL.
   - UK under-18 rule: a DevForum snippet says experiences declaring paid random items without calling the PolicyService API are unavailable to UK users under 18. I did not open that page, so treat as unverified. Source (search result only): https://devforum.roblox.com/t/update-on-paid-random-items-restriction-for-uk-users-under-18/3072183
   - Blocked countries: the official page says regulation varies by country and does not list them. UNKNOWN which countries.
   - Subscriptions, regional pricing, price optimisation tool, Rewarded Video / Immersive Ads eligibility: UNKNOWN (not opened).
   - Creator Rewards: replaced Engagement Based Payouts and Creator Affiliate programs. Daily Engagement pays 5 Robux per qualifying Active Spender per day (10+ min play, one of the first three experiences launched that day), and applies to all creators from July 24 2025. Audience Expansion pays a 35% share of the first $100 of a new user's purchases in their first 60 days, for ID-verified creators. Source: https://create.roblox.com/docs/en-us/creator-rewards.md
   - Premium Payouts: the Creator Rewards page does not mention it. A wiki says it was replaced July 24 2025 (https://roblox.fandom.com/wiki/Creator_Rewards, search result, not opened). Treat as likely but unconfirmed.
   - DevEx rate: sources conflict. $0.0038 per Robux from Sept 5 2025 is reported by search snippets; another blog says $0.0035. A report of a 42% increase is unverified. Do not budget on any of these until checked on the official DevEx page (not opened).

### E. Conclusion (one page)
- Gap: the idle collect loop is proven at scale (Throw Food For Animals about 200M+ visits in ~8 weeks; Steal a Brainrot 25.8M CCU peak). The anime-character version is thin: one small throw-anime title (2K peak, 1.1M visits) and several unverified collect-anime titles. The gap is an anime-styled throw/collect game with an original roster and stronger onboarding and presentation. Evidence of the gap is thin because I could not get stats for most anime titles.
- Risk 1, IP: recognisable anime characters carry takedown risk, and Roblox does not reopen a DMCA-removed game without consent. Use original characters with an anime art style, and do not use real titles, names or logos.
- Risk 2, policy: paid random items (the lootbox or egg-style "chance to get a character" mechanic) must show all odds before purchase, and under ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted must have a fallback. Design the gacha so it can run in restricted mode, and test the PolicyService flag.
- Risk 3, market saturation and cost: the top collect games have 15 to 400K+ CCU and years of content. The genre's sales data (what sells) is unverified. Do not plan on Robux revenue numbers without a developer data source.
- Features most likely to lift D1 retention and ARPPU (reasoned from the evidence, not measured): (a) a fast first-collect reveal in the first 2 minutes (rarity reveal, the Steal a Brainrot pattern), (b) offline earnings so a session ends with income waiting, (c) daily reward and playtime reward to create a return reason, (d) a visible collection index with completion goals, (e) limited-time events with exclusive characters, (f) a paid random item that discloses odds (the lootbox must comply, see Risk 2), and (g) a non-random direct purchase (a specific character at a fixed price) as the simpler ARPPU path, since random purchases are restricted in some cases. None of these were benchmarked against sales data.

## What the niche is missing
- An anime-styled, original-character collect loop with a real onboarding path. Only small anime-collect titles were found, and no stats for most of them.
- Evidence on what sells (passes vs products) is missing for the whole genre.

## Build cost signals
(The thing most likely to blow an eight-hour budget)
- Compliance and gating: the paid-random-item disclosure UI, the ArePaidRandomItemsRestricted fallback, and the UK under-18 path must all be built and tested before publish. This is likely the hidden cost.
- Art: anime-style R15 characters with auras need a consistent original roster. Free Creator Store models carry IP risk, so each must be verified or replaced. Budget time for asset provenance.
- Live ops: the genre leans on weekly events and limited items. Plan for at least one event scaffold in the first build.
- Economy tuning (rebirth, offline income, luck) is where playtest iteration eats time. Not measured.
